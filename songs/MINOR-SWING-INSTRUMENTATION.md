# Minor Swing - Instrumentation Mapping

**How the original 1937 quintet maps to our sextet arrangement**

---

## Original Quintette du Hot Club de France (1937)

1. **Django Reinhardt** - Lead/solo guitar
2. **Stéphane Grappelli** - Violin (melody)
3. **Joseph Reinhardt** - Rhythm guitar (la pompe)
4. **Eugène Vées** - Rhythm guitar (la pompe)
5. **Louis Vola** - Double bass (pizzicato)

**Key characteristic**: All-string ensemble, NO drums, NO piano

---

## Detailed Role Breakdown

### 1. Rhythm Guitar (La Pompe)

**Original role**: Foundation rhythm (replaced drums in gypsy jazz)  
**Our arrangement**: IDENTICAL - keep la pompe pattern  
**Why essential**: Without this, it's not gypsy jazz!

**Pattern:**

```
| ↓  ↑↓  ↓  ↑↓ |
  1   2   3   4
  død HIT død HIT
```

- Beats 2 & 4 are percussive, sharp, muted immediately
- This creates the driving "chick-CHUNK" feel

---

### 2. Saxophone

**Combines roles from:**

- **Grappelli's violin** (primary melodic voice)
- **Django's lead lines** (some melodic passages)

**What sax does:**

- Statement of head melody (based on Am6 arpeggios)
- Solo choruses (like Grappelli)
- Call-and-response with piano (like Django/Grappelli dialogue)
- Upper register playing (bright, violin-like tone)

**Style notes:**

- NOT bebop sax style
- Think "violin with a reed"
- Arpeggio-based lines, not scale runs
- Controlled vibrato
- Study Hubert Rostaing (clarinet who replaced Grappelli)

---

### 3. Piano

**Replaces:**

- **Django's lead/solo guitar** parts

**What piano does:**

- **During HEAD**: Sparse comping (shell voicings on beats 2 & 4)
- **During PIANO SOLO**: Takes Django's improvisation role
- **During SAX SOLO**: Lays out or very minimal
- **Fills & transitions**: Arpeggio runs like Django

**Key difference from typical jazz piano:**

- Much SPARSER - leave space!
- Think like a guitarist, not a pianist
- Use Django's voicings (6th chords, major 6/9)
- Shell voicings (root + 6th or root + 7th)

**Voicings to use:**

```
Am6:  A + F# (root + 6th)
Dm6:  D + B  (root + 6th)
E7:   E + D  (root + 7th)
Bb7:  Bb + Ab (root + 7th)
```

---

### 4. Standup Bass

**Original role**: Louis Vola's walking bass  
**Our arrangement**: VERY SIMILAR

**Keep these elements:**

- Pizzicato (plucked) throughout
- Walking basslines
- Root on beat 1 (always!)
- Chromatic approaches between chords
- Occasional two-feel (during head melody)

**Original style preserved** ✅

---

### 5. Drums (NEW - Not in Original)

**Challenge**: Original had NO drums!  
**Solution**: Keep drums LIGHT and SUPPORTIVE

**Approach:**

- **BRUSHES throughout** (not sticks)
- Emphasize **hi-hat foot on 2 & 4** (simulates la pompe accents)
- **Minimal bass drum** (beat 1 only, or none)
- Ghost notes on snare (barely audible)
- Think "whisper drums" - support, don't dominate

**Critical**: Drums must NOT overpower the la pompe rhythm guitar!

---

### 6. Percussion for Singer (NEW)

**Instruments:**

- **Tambourine** - taps on beats 2 & 4
- **Shaker** - light eighth notes (background texture)
- **Woodblock** - accents (intro, ending, breaks)

**Role:**

- Reinforces beats 2 & 4 (with la pompe)
- Adds color without complexity
- Singer can play while not singing

**Pattern (tambourine):**

```
         1   +   2   +   3   +   4   +
Tamb:    .   .   X   .   .   .   X   .
```

Simple, supportive, reinforces swing feel.

## The Two Lead Voices Concept

### Original: Django + Grappelli

Django and Stéphane created magical **call-and-response dialogues**:

- Trading melodic phrases
- Complementary improvisation
- One plays while other rests
- Then reverse roles

### Our Version: Piano + Saxophone

We recreate this with piano and sax:

**During HEAD:**

- Sax states melody
- Piano supports lightly

**During SOLOS:**

- Piano solo (choruses 3-4): Sax lays out
- Sax solo (choruses 5-6): Piano lays out
- Creating space for each voice

**Call-and-response:**

- Short traded phrases
- Complementary lines
- Never both playing busy at same time

## What We're Adding (Not in Original)

### Drums

- **Why add**: Modern audiences expect drums in swing
- **How to keep authentic**: Brushes, light touch, support la pompe
- **Risk**: Could sound too "American swing" vs gypsy jazz
- **Mitigation**: Keep minimal, emphasize 2 & 4

### Piano

- **Why add**: Provides harmonic support and solo voice
- **How to keep authentic**: Sparse comping, Django-style voicings
- **Risk**: Could make texture too dense
- **Mitigation**: Lay out during sections, beats 2 & 4 only

### Percussion

- **Why add**: Engagement for singer, reinforces rhythm
- **How to keep authentic**: Simple patterns, 2 & 4 emphasis
- **Risk**: Could clutter the sound
- **Mitigation**: Keep minimal, blend with rhythm section

## What We're Keeping (From Original)

✅ **La pompe rhythm guitar** - The essential foundation  
✅ **Pizzicato walking bass** - Original Louis Vola style  
✅ **A minor tonality** - Modal, minor-key feel  
✅ **Fast tempo** (but 210, not 240 BPM)  
✅ **Arpeggio-based improvisation** - Django's vocabulary  
✅ **Transparent texture** - Space and lightness  
✅ **Call-and-response** - Between lead voices  
✅ **Spontaneous feel** - Not over-arranged

---

## What We're Changing

🔄 **Violin → Saxophone** - Different timbre, but similar role  
🔄 **Lead guitar → Piano** - For solo sections  
➕ **Adding drums** - Light, supportive only  
➕ **Adding percussion** - For rhythmic reinforcement  
📊 **Fuller texture** - More instruments than original quintet

---

## The Hybrid Approach

We're creating a **"Gypsy Jazz meets Swing Sextet"** arrangement:

**From Gypsy Jazz:**

- La pompe rhythm guitar (essential!)
- Arpeggio-based improvisation
- Minor-key modal feel
- Transparent texture
- Fast tempo

**From American Swing:**

- Addition of drums
- Piano voicings/comping
- Fuller instrumentation
- Some jazz vocabulary

**Result:**
A respectful adaptation that honors Django's original while making it work for a modern swing band suitable for lindy hop dancing.

## Solo Section Distribution

### Chorus 1-2: HEAD (Melody)

- **Lead**: Saxophone (Grappelli's role)
- **Support**: Rhythm guitar (la pompe), bass, light drums
- **Piano**: Minimal (beats 2 & 4 only)

### Chorus 3-4: PIANO SOLO

- **Lead**: Piano (Django's solo guitar role)
- **Support**: Full rhythm section
- **Sax**: Lays out completely

### Chorus 5-6: SAX SOLO

- **Lead**: Saxophone (Grappelli + Django combined)
- **Support**: Rhythm guitar, bass, drums
- **Piano**: Lays out or very minimal

### Chorus 7: HEAD OUT

- **Lead**: Saxophone (return to melody)
- **Support**: Full band
- **Piano**: Returns to beats 2 & 4

## Key to Success

### 1. Rhythm Guitar MUST be strong

The la pompe is what makes this gypsy jazz. Without it, you just have a swing sextet playing "Minor Swing." WITH it, you have authentic gypsy jazz adapted for modern swing.

### 2. Leave SPACE

Don't have everyone playing all the time. The original quintet was transparent - you could hear each instrument. Keep that feel even with more instruments.

### 3. Study the ORIGINAL

Listen to Django's 1937 recording repeatedly:

- How do Django and Grappelli interact?
- When does bass play two-feel vs walking?
- What's the actual tempo?
- How light is the touch?

### 4. Think DIALOGUE not BACKING

Piano and sax should CONVERSE, not just play over changes. Listen to each other, respond, trade ideas.

### 5. Beats 2 & 4 are EVERYTHING

- La pompe accents: 2 & 4
- Hi-hat foot: 2 & 4
- Piano chords (during head): 2 & 4
- Tambourine: 2 & 4

This drives the swing feel!

## Summary

**We're NOT removing the gypsy jazz elements** - we're ADDING to them:

```
Original (1937):
Rhythm Guitars + Lead Guitar + Violin + Bass
                ↓
Our Arrangement:
Rhythm Guitar + Piano + Sax + Bass + Drums + Percussion
      ↓            ↓       ↓
   (Keep!)    (Django) (Grappelli)
```

The la pompe rhythm guitar is the bridge between authentic gypsy jazz and modern swing arrangement. Keep it strong, and everything else falls into place!
